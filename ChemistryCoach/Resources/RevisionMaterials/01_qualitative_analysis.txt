During the practical exam, use approximately 1 cm depth of solution (1-2 cm3)
for each test and add reagents slowly, ensuring good mixing, until no further
change is seen.

SECTION 1: CATION TESTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two main reagents are used: aqueous sodium hydroxide (NaOH) and aqueous
ammonia (NH3). Add the reagent slowly, then add excess.

CATION     | NaOH (add, then excess)           | NH3 (add, then excess)
-----------+-----------------------------------+----------------------------------
NH4+       | Gas produced on warming; gas      | (Use NaOH test instead)
(ammonium) | turns damp red litmus blue         |
           |                                   |
Ca2+       | White precipitate; insoluble in    | No precipitate
(calcium)  | excess NaOH                        |
           |                                   |
Cu2+       | Light blue precipitate; insoluble  | Light blue precipitate; dissolves
(copper)   | in excess NaOH                     | in excess NH3 to form deep blue
           |                                   | solution
           |                                   |
Fe2+       | Green precipitate; insoluble in    | Green precipitate; insoluble in
(iron II)  | excess; turns brown on standing    | excess; turns brown on standing
           |                                   |
Fe3+       | Reddish-brown precipitate;         | Reddish-brown precipitate;
(iron III) | insoluble in excess                | insoluble in excess
           |                                   |
Zn2+       | White precipitate; dissolves in    | White precipitate; dissolves in
(zinc)     | excess NaOH to form colourless     | excess NH3 to form colourless
           | solution                           | solution
           |                                   |
Al3+       | White precipitate; dissolves in    | White precipitate; insoluble in
(aluminium)| excess NaOH to form colourless     | excess NH3
           | solution                           |
           |                                   |
Pb2+       | White precipitate; dissolves in    | White precipitate; dissolves in
(lead)     | excess NaOH                        | excess NH3

KEY DISTINCTIONS:
- Al3+ vs Zn2+: Both give white ppt with NaOH, soluble in excess.
  DISTINGUISHER: Add excess NH3. Al3+ gives white ppt INSOLUBLE in excess NH3.
  Zn2+ gives white ppt SOLUBLE in excess NH3.
- Ca2+: White ppt with NaOH (insoluble in excess) but NO ppt with NH3.
- NH4+: No precipitate. Warm with NaOH; test gas with damp red litmus.

               |                                  |

and gives a false positive for Cl-.

SECTION 4: FLAME TESTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Clean a platinum or nichrome wire by dipping in concentrated hydrochloric acid
and heating in a non-luminous Bunsen flame until no colour is seen. Moisten the
wire with acid, dip into the sample, and hold in the flame.

ION        | FLAME COLOUR
-----------+------------------
Li+        | Crimson red
(lithium)  |
Na+        | Bright yellow (intense, persistent)
(sodium)   |
K+         | Lilac / pale violet
(potassium)|
Ca2+       | Orange-red
(calcium)  |
Ba2+       | Apple green / pale green
(barium)   |
Cu2+       | Blue-green
(copper)   |

MNEMONIC: "Little Naughty Kids Can Be Clever"
Li (crimson) -> Na (yellow) -> K (lilac) -> Ca (orange-red) -> Ba (green) -> Cu (blue-green)

SECTION 5: INDICATORS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INDICATOR        | pH RANGE  | ACID COLOUR    | ALKALI COLOUR
-----------------+-----------+----------------+------------------
Methyl orange    | 3.1 - 4.4 | Red            | Yellow
Phenolphthalein  | 8.2 - 10.0| Colourless      | Pink
Universal        | 1 - 14   | Red (pH 1)     | Violet (pH 14)
Indicator        |           |                | (full spectrum)

CHOOSING AN INDICATOR FOR TITRATION:
- Strong acid + strong alkali: phenolphthalein OR methyl orange
- Strong acid + weak alkali: methyl orange (endpoint in acidic pH range)
- Weak acid + strong alkali: phenolphthalein (endpoint in basic pH range)
- Universal Indicator is NOT suitable for titrations (gradual colour change)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6: COMMON QA MISTAKES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Writing an inference as an observation. "Chloride ion present" is an
   inference. "White precipitate formed" is an observation. Record what you
   SEE first, then state the inference.

2. Using hydrochloric acid to acidify before adding silver nitrate. HCl
   introduces Cl- ions and gives a false white precipitate. Always use
   dilute NITRIC acid.

3. Not testing for solubility in excess. Al3+ and Zn2+ both give white ppt
   with NaOH. You MUST add excess to distinguish (Al3+ dissolves in excess
   NaOH; Zn2+ also dissolves in excess NaOH — use NH3 to distinguish them).

4. Saying "bubbles form" without identifying or testing the gas. For
   carbonate, you must test the gas with limewater.

5. Claiming a single ion without a confirmatory test. A white precipitate
   with NaOH could be Ca2+, Zn2+, Al3+, or Pb2+. Always do the excess test
   and the NH3 test before concluding.

6. Not warming with NaOH for the ammonium test. The gas (NH3) is only
   released on warming.

7. For nitrate test: forgetting to add aluminium foil AND NaOH together.
   Both are needed.
