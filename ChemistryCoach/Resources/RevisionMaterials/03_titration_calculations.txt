================================================================================
TITRATION CALCULATIONS — WORKED EXAMPLES
Singapore GCE O-Level Chemistry (6092)
================================================================================

KEY FORMULAS:
  moles = concentration x volume(dm3)
  concentration = moles / volume(dm3)
  volume(dm3) = volume(cm3) / 1000
  titre = final burette reading - initial burette reading

ALWAYS:
  - Write the balanced equation first.
  - Convert cm3 to dm3 by dividing by 1000.
  - Use the mole ratio from the balanced equation.
  - Carry units through every step.

BURETTE TECHNIQUE:
  - Record readings to 2 decimal places (nearest 0.05 cm3).
  - Concordant titres are within 0.20 cm3 of each other (SEAB guidance).
  - Average only the concordant titres. Discard anomalous results.
  - Rinse the burette with the solution it will contain (not water).

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 1: Finding concentration of NaOH (1:1 ratio)
================================================================================

Question:
  25.0 cm3 of NaOH solution was neutralised by 22.4 cm3 of 0.100 mol/dm3 HCl.
  Find the concentration of NaOH.

Step 1: Equation
  HCl + NaOH -> NaCl + H2O

Step 2: Moles of HCl (the known solution)
  Volume of HCl = 22.4 cm3 = 22.4/1000 = 0.0224 dm3
  Moles of HCl = 0.100 x 0.0224 = 0.00224 mol

Step 3: Mole ratio
  HCl : NaOH = 1 : 1
  Moles of NaOH = 0.00224 mol

Step 4: Concentration of NaOH
  Volume of NaOH = 25.0 cm3 = 0.0250 dm3
  Concentration = 0.00224 / 0.0250 = 0.0896 mol/dm3

Answer: 0.0896 mol/dm3

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 2: Sulfuric acid titration (1:2 ratio) + concordance
================================================================================

Question:
  A student titrates H2SO4 against 25.0 cm3 of 0.200 mol/dm3 NaOH.
  Titration results: 18.60, 18.55, 18.90, 18.50 cm3.
  Find the concentration of H2SO4.

Step 1: Identify concordant titres
  18.60, 18.55, and 18.50 are within 0.20 cm3 of each other.
  18.90 is anomalous — discard it.

Step 2: Mean titre
  Mean = (18.60 + 18.55 + 18.50) / 3 = 55.65 / 3 = 18.55 cm3

Step 3: Equation
  H2SO4 + 2NaOH -> Na2SO4 + 2H2O

Step 4: Moles of NaOH
  Volume = 25.0 cm3 = 0.0250 dm3
  Moles of NaOH = 0.200 x 0.0250 = 0.00500 mol

Step 5: Mole ratio
  H2SO4 : NaOH = 1 : 2
  Moles of H2SO4 = 0.00500 / 2 = 0.00250 mol

Step 6: Concentration of H2SO4
  Volume = 18.55 cm3 = 0.01855 dm3
  Concentration = 0.00250 / 0.01855 = 0.135 mol/dm3

Answer: 0.135 mol/dm3

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 3: Finding volume of acid required (2:1 ratio)
================================================================================

Question:
  Calculate the volume of 0.250 mol/dm3 HNO3 required to react completely
  with 40.0 cm3 of 0.150 mol/dm3 Ca(OH)2.

Step 1: Equation
  2HNO3 + Ca(OH)2 -> Ca(NO3)2 + 2H2O

Step 2: Moles of Ca(OH)2
  Volume = 40.0 cm3 = 0.0400 dm3
  Moles of Ca(OH)2 = 0.150 x 0.0400 = 0.00600 mol

Step 3: Mole ratio
  HNO3 : Ca(OH)2 = 2 : 1
  Moles of HNO3 = 2 x 0.00600 = 0.0120 mol

Step 4: Volume of HNO3
  Volume = moles / concentration = 0.0120 / 0.250 = 0.0480 dm3
  Volume = 0.0480 x 1000 = 48.0 cm3

Answer: 48.0 cm3

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 4: Finding mass of salt formed
================================================================================

Question:
  25.0 cm3 of 0.500 mol/dm3 NaOH reacts with excess HCl.
  Calculate the mass of NaCl formed.
  (Mr: Na = 23, Cl = 35.5)

Step 1: Equation
  HCl + NaOH -> NaCl + H2O

Step 2: Moles of NaOH
  Volume = 25.0 cm3 = 0.0250 dm3
  Moles = 0.500 x 0.0250 = 0.0125 mol

Step 3: Mole ratio
  NaOH : NaCl = 1 : 1
  Moles of NaCl = 0.0125 mol

Step 4: Mass of NaCl
  Mr of NaCl = 23 + 35.5 = 58.5
  Mass = moles x Mr = 0.0125 x 58.5 = 0.731 g

Answer: 0.731 g

================================================================================
EXAMPLE 5: Multi-step — titre to mass of solid
================================================================================

Question:
  In a titration, 23.50 cm3 of 0.105 mol/dm3 H2SO4 neutralised 25.0 cm3 of
  NaOH. Calculate the mass of Na2SO4 that could be obtained from 500 cm3
  of this NaOH solution.
  (Mr: Na = 23, S = 32, O = 16)

Step 1: Equation
  H2SO4 + 2NaOH -> Na2SO4 + 2H2O

Step 2: Moles of H2SO4
  Volume = 23.50 cm3 = 0.02350 dm3
  Moles of H2SO4 = 0.105 x 0.02350 = 0.002468 mol

Step 3: Mole ratio -> moles of NaOH in 25.0 cm3
  H2SO4 : NaOH = 1 : 2
  Moles of NaOH in 25.0 cm3 = 2 x 0.002468 = 0.004935 mol

Step 4: Concentration of NaOH
  Concentration = 0.004935 / 0.0250 = 0.1974 mol/dm3

Step 5: Moles of NaOH in 500 cm3
  Volume = 500 cm3 = 0.500 dm3
  Moles = 0.1974 x 0.500 = 0.0987 mol

Step 6: Mole ratio -> moles of Na2SO4
  NaOH : Na2SO4 = 2 : 1
  Moles of Na2SO4 = 0.0987 / 2 = 0.04935 mol

Step 7: Mass of Na2SO4
  Mr of Na2SO4 = (2 x 23) + 32 + (4 x 16) = 46 + 32 + 64 = 142
  Mass = 0.04935 x 142 = 7.01 g

Answer: 7.01 g

================================================================================
TITRATION TECHNIQUE — COMMON ERRORS
================================================================================

1. Not rinsing the burette with the solution it will contain. Residual water
   dilutes the solution, giving a larger titre and a lower calculated
   concentration.

2. Reading the burette from above or below eye level (parallax error).
   Read at eye level, using the bottom of the meniscus.

3. Including a rough (trial) titre in the average. Only average concordant
   titres.

4. Forgetting to convert cm3 to dm3 (or dividing by 1000).

5. Using the wrong mole ratio. Always write the balanced equation first.

6. Not swirling the conical flask during titration. Uneven mixing means the
   endpoint may appear earlier or later than the true equivalence point.

7. Adding titrant too quickly near the endpoint. Add dropwise near the
   colour change.

8. Using Universal Indicator for a titration. Use methyl orange or
   phenolphthalein — they have a sharp colour change at the endpoint.
