TOPIC 1: EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
================================================================================

- Scientific method: observe, hypothesise, test, conclude.
- Variables: independent (what you change), dependent (what you measure),
  controlled (what you keep constant).
- Fair test: only one variable changed at a time; all others controlled.
- Accuracy: how close to the true value.
- Precision: how close repeated measurements are to each other.
- Reliability: reproducibility of results (repeat and average).
- Anomalies: outliers that do not fit the pattern; discard and identify.

Paper 3 technique standards:
- Burette readings to 0.05 cm3 (2 decimal places).
- QA aliquots: 1-2 cm3 of solution; add reagents slowly.
- Significant figures: match the least precise measurement.
- Tables: independent variable in left column; dependent in right.
- Graphs: label axes with quantity and units; choose a scale that uses
  more than half the grid; plot points as small crosses or dots.

TOPIC 2: SEPARATION TECHNIQUES
================================================================================

(See file 08_separation_techniques.txt for full details.)

Key points:
- Filtration: insoluble solid + liquid.
- Crystallisation: dissolved solid from solution (solid may decompose).
- Evaporation to dryness: dissolved solid (does NOT decompose).
- Simple distillation: liquid from solution.
- Fractional distillation: miscible liquids (close boiling points).
- Separating funnel: immiscible liquids.
- Sublimation: sublimable solid from non-sublimable.
- Chromatography: dissolved dyes; Rf = distance substance / distance solvent.

TOPIC 3: ACIDS, BASES AND SALTS
================================================================================

ACIDS: substances that produce H+ ions in water. pH < 7. Turn blue litmus red.
  - Strong acids: fully ionise (HCl, HNO3, H2SO4).
  - Weak acids: partially ionise (ethanoic acid, carbonic acid).

BASES: substances that neutralise acids. Metal oxides and hydroxides.
  - Alkalis: bases that dissolve in water, producing OH- ions. pH > 7.
    Turn red litmus blue. Examples: NaOH, KOH, NH3(aq).
  - Strong alkalis: fully ionise (NaOH, KOH).
  - Weak alkalis: partially ionise (NH3).

NEUTRALISATION: acid + base -> salt + water.
  H+ + OH- -> H2O

pH SCALE: 0-14. pH 7 = neutral. pH < 7 = acidic. pH > 7 = alkaline.
  Measured with Universal Indicator or pH meter.

SALT PREPARATION: (See file 02_solubility_and_salts.txt)
  - Insoluble salt -> Precipitation
  - Soluble SPA salt -> Titration
  - Other soluble salt -> Excess insoluble base method

TOPIC 4: OXIDATION AND REDUCTION
================================================================================

OXIDATION: gain of oxygen, OR loss of hydrogen, OR loss of electrons.
REDUCTION: loss of oxygen, OR gain of hydrogen, OR gain of electrons.

OIL RIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).

REDOX REACTIONS: both oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously.
  - The substance oxidised = reducing agent.
  - The substance reduced = oxidising agent.

REACTIVITY SERIES (most to least reactive):
  K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, [H], Cu, Ag, Au

Metals above H react with dilute acids to produce H2.
Metals below H do NOT react with dilute acids.

Metal displacement: a more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal
from its compound.
  e.g. Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu (zinc displaces copper)
  e.g. Fe + CuSO4 -> FeSO4 + Cu (iron displaces copper)

RUSTING: iron + oxygen + water -> hydrated iron(III) oxide.
  Prevention: painting, oiling, galvanising (zinc coating), sacrificial
  protection (attach a more reactive metal like Mg).

TOPIC 5: GASES IN THE ATMOSPHERE
================================================================================

Composition of air: N2 (78%), O2 (21%), Ar (0.9%), CO2 (0.04%).
Fractional distillation of liquid air separates these by boiling point.

TESTS FOR GASES (see file 01_qualitative_analysis.txt):
  H2: pop with lighted splint.
  O2: relights glowing splint.
  CO2: turns limewater milky.
  NH3: pungent; turns damp red litmus blue.
  Cl2: greenish-yellow; bleaches damp blue litmus.
  SO2: turns acidified KMnO4 from purple to colourless.

CARBON CYCLE: photosynthesis (removes CO2), respiration (adds CO2),
  combustion (adds CO2), decomposition (adds CO2).

GLOBAL WARMING: increased CO2 from burning fossil fuels enhances the
  greenhouse effect. Consequences: rising temperatures, melting ice caps,
  rising sea levels, climate change.

PREVENTING GLOBAL WARMING: reduce fossil fuel use, use renewable energy,
  plant trees, carbon capture.

TOPIC 6: ATOMIC STRUCTURE AND STOICHIOMETRY
================================================================================

ATOM: nucleus (protons +, neutrons neutral) + electron shells (electrons -).
  Proton number (Z) = number of protons = number of electrons (in neutral atom).
  Nucleon number (A) = protons + neutrons.
  Isotopes: same protons, different neutrons (e.g. Cl-35 and Cl-37).

ELECTRON CONFIGURATION (first 20 elements):
  K shell: max 2 electrons
  L shell: max 8 electrons
  M shell: max 8 electrons (for first 20)
  e.g. Na (11): 2.8.1
  e.g. Cl (17): 2.8.7
  e.g. Ar (18): 2.8.8

IONS: atoms gain/lose electrons to achieve a full outer shell.
  Metal atoms LOSE electrons -> positive ions (cations).
  Non-metal atoms GAIN electrons -> negative ions (anions).

MOLE CONCEPT:
  1 mole = 6.02 x 10^23 particles (Avogadro's number).
  Moles = mass / Mr.
  Moles = concentration x volume(dm3).
  Moles of gas = volume(dm3) / 24 (at room temperature and pressure).
  1 mole of any gas occupies 24 dm3 at rtp.

EMPIRICAL FORMULA: simplest whole number ratio of atoms in a compound.
  1. Find moles of each element (mass / Ar).
  2. Divide by the smallest.
  3. Find the simplest ratio.

MOLECULAR FORMULA: actual number of atoms in a molecule.
  = empirical formula x n, where n = Mr(empirical) / Mr(molecular).

TOPIC 7: CHEMICAL BONDING
================================================================================

IONIC BONDING: transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal. Electrostatic
  attraction between oppositely charged ions. Forms giant ionic lattice.
  Properties: high melting/boiling point, soluble in water, conducts
  electricity when molten or in solution (ions free to move), brittle.
  Examples: NaCl, MgO, CaCl2.

COVALENT BONDING: sharing of electron pairs between non-metal atoms.
  Simple molecular: low melting/boiling point, poor conductors.
    Examples: H2O, CO2, NH3, CH4, Cl2.
  Macromolecular (giant covalent): very high melting point.
    Examples: diamond (each C bonded to 4 others), graphite (layers, conducts
    electricity, soft/slippery), silicon dioxide (SiO2).

METALLIC BONDING: positive metal ions in a "sea" of delocalised electrons.
  Properties: good conductors (free electrons), malleable and ductile,
  high melting point (varies).

INTERMOLECULAR FORCES:
  - Van der Waals (weakest): between simple molecules.
  - Hydrogen bonding (stronger): between H and O/N/F in different molecules.
  - Ionic/electrostatic (strongest): in ionic lattices.

TOPIC 8: CHEMICAL ENERGETICS
================================================================================

(See file 05_energy_changes.txt for full details.)

Key points:
- Exothermic: heat released, Delta H negative, surroundings get hotter.
- Endothermic: heat absorbed, Delta H positive, surroundings get colder.
- Bond breaking = endothermic. Bond making = exothermic.
- Calorimetry: q = m x c x Delta T.
- Fuel cells: convert chemical energy to electrical energy. Hydrogen fuel
  cell: H2 + O2 -> H2O, produces electricity and water.

TOPIC 9: CHEMICAL REACTIONS
================================================================================

TYPES OF REACTION:
  - Combination: A + B -> C (e.g. 2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO)
  - Decomposition: C -> A + B (e.g. CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2)
  - Displacement: A + BC -> AC + B (e.g. Zn + CuSO4 -> ZnSO4 + Cu)
  - Neutralisation: acid + base -> salt + water
  - Combustion: substance + O2 -> oxides (+ energy)
  - Precipitation: two solutions -> insoluble solid + solution

RATE OF REACTION (see file 06_rate_of_reaction.txt):
  - Factors: temperature, concentration, surface area, catalyst.
  - Collision theory: effective collisions need sufficient energy and
    correct orientation.
  - Catalyst: provides alternative pathway with lower activation energy.
    Not consumed. Remains chemically unchanged.
  - Examples: MnO2 catalyses decomposition of H2O2. Iron catalyses Haber
    process. Vanadium(V) oxide catalyses Contact process.

REVERSIBLE REACTIONS:
  - Can go in both directions (forward and backward).
  - Dynamic equilibrium: rate of forward = rate of backward; concentrations
    constant.
  - Le Chatelier's principle: if conditions change, equilibrium shifts to
    oppose the change.

HABER PROCESS (industrial):
  N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 (exothermic)
  Conditions: 200 atm, 450C, iron catalyst.
  Compromise: high pressure favours forward (fewer moles of gas); high
  temperature favours backward (exothermic). 450C is a compromise between
  rate and yield.

CONTACT PROCESS (industrial):
  2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3 (exothermic)
  Conditions: 2 atm, 450C, V2O5 catalyst.
  SO3 then dissolved in conc. H2SO4 to form oleum (H2S2O7), then diluted.

TOPIC 10: ELECTROLYSIS
================================================================================

(See file 04_electrolysis.txt for full details.)

Key points:
- Cathode (-): reduction. Cations attracted. Metal or H2 discharged.
- Anode (+): oxidation. Anions attracted. Halide or O2 discharged.
- Molten: only ions from the compound. Straightforward prediction.
- Aqueous: H+ and OH- from water compete.
  Cathode: metal below H -> metal discharged. Metal above H -> H2.
  Anode: concentrated halide -> halogen. Otherwise -> O2 from OH-.
- Half-equations: balance atoms, then charge with electrons.
- Applications: electroplating, extraction of aluminium, purification of
  copper.

ELECTROPLATING:
  - Object to be plated = cathode.
  - Plating metal = anode.
  - Electrolyte = salt of plating metal (e.g. AgNO3 for silver plating).
  - Metal ions deposit on cathode object.

PURIFICATION OF COPPER:
  - Impure copper = anode.
  - Pure copper = cathode.
  - Electrolyte = CuSO4 solution.
  - Cu dissolves from anode, deposits on cathode. Impurities fall as "anode
    sludge."

TOPIC 11: ORGANIC CHEMISTRY
================================================================================

ALKANES (CnH2n+2): saturated (single bonds only). Unreactive. Combustible.
  e.g. CH4 (methane), C2H6 (ethane), C3H8 (propane).
  Combustion: CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O.

ALKENES (CnH2n): unsaturated (contain C=C double bond). More reactive.
  e.g. C2H4 (ethene), C3H6 (propene).
  Test for unsaturation: decolourises bromine water (orange -> colourless).
  Addition reactions: H2, Br2, H2O add across the double bond.

ALCOHOLS (CnH2n+1OH): functional group -OH.
  e.g. CH3OH (methanol), C2H5OH (ethanol).
  Ethanol: produced by fermentation (glucose -> ethanol + CO2, yeast, 37C).
  Combustion: C2H5OH + 3O2 -> 2CO2 + 3H2O.
  Oxidation: ethanol -> ethanoic acid (using acidified KMnO4).

CARBOXYLIC ACIDS (CnH2n+1COOH): functional group -COOH.
  e.g. HCOOH (methanoic acid), CH3COOH (ethanoic acid).
  Weak acids. React with metals, bases, carbonates (like other acids).
  Ethanoic acid + ethanol -> ethyl ethanoate + water (esterification, with
  conc. H2SO4 catalyst).

MACROMOLECULES:
  - Polythene: addition polymer from ethene. (n CH2=CH2 -> -(CH2-CH2)n-)
  - Nylon: condensation polymer. Formed from a diamine and a dicarboxylic
    acid. Water eliminated.
  - Proteins: natural condensation polymers of amino acids. Peptide bonds.

NATURAL MACROMOLECULES:
  - Proteins: made of amino acids. Essential for growth and repair.
  - Starch: polymer of glucose. Energy storage in plants.
  - Cellulose: polymer of glucose. Structural in plant cell walls.

TOPIC 12: PERIODIC TABLE
================================================================================

GROUP I (Alkali Metals): Li, Na, K. Very reactive (1 electron in outer shell).
  React with water: 2Na + 2H2O -> 2NaOH + H2. Reactivity increases down group.
  Soft, low density, stored under oil (to prevent reaction with air/moisture).

GROUP VII (Halogens): F, Cl, Br, I. 7 electrons in outer shell. Diatomic (Cl2).
  Reactivity decreases down group (F most reactive).
  Displacement: more reactive halogen displaces less reactive from its salt.
    e.g. Cl2 + 2KBr -> 2KCl + Br2 (chlorine displaces bromine).
  Colours: Cl2 = pale green, Br2 = red-brown, I2 = purple/black solid.

GROUP 0 (Noble Gases): He, Ne, Ar, Kr. Full outer shell. Unreactive (inert).
  Used in lighting (neon signs), argon in light bulbs, helium in balloons.

TRANSITION ELEMENTS: between Groups II and III. Form coloured compounds
  (e.g. Cu2+ = blue, Fe2+ = green, Fe3+ = brown). Often used as catalysts
  (Fe in Haber process, Ni in hydrogenation, V2O5 in Contact process).
  Have variable oxidation states (e.g. Fe2+ and Fe3+).
