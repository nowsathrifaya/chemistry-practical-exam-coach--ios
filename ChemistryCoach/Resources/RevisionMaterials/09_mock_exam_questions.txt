QUESTION 1: TITRATION (MMO + PDO + ACE) — 12 marks
================================================================================

A student carries out a titration to find the concentration of a solution of
sulfuric acid, H2SO4, using a standard solution of sodium hydroxide, NaOH.

(a) Describe how the student should prepare 250 cm3 of 0.100 mol/dm3 NaOH
    solution from solid sodium hydroxide. [3]

(b) The student uses 25.0 cm3 of the NaOH solution in each titration.
    Describe how to measure this volume accurately. [1]

(c) The student records the following titration results:

    | Titration | Rough | 1st Accurate | 2nd Accurate | 3rd Accurate |
    |-----------+-------+-------------+-------------+-------------|
    | Final (cm3) | 23.50 | 23.20 | 23.15 | 23.30 |
    | Initial (cm3)| 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

    (i) Identify the concordant titres. [1]
    (ii) Calculate the mean titre. [1]

(d) The equation for the reaction is:
    H2SO4 + 2NaOH -> Na2SO4 + 2H2O

    Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid. [4]

(e) Suggest two sources of error in this titration and how each could be
    minimised. [2]

--- ANSWERS ---

(a) Any three of:
    - Weigh 1.00 g of solid NaOH using a balance (Mr NaOH = 40; 0.100 x 0.250
      x 40 = 1.00 g).
    - Dissolve in a small amount of distilled water in a beaker.
    - Transfer to a 250 cm3 volumetric flask, washing the beaker with
      distilled water into the flask.
    - Add distilled water to the mark.
    - Stopper and invert to mix.

(b) Use a pipette (25.0 cm3) with a pipette filler.

(c)(i) Concordant titres: 23.20 and 23.15 cm3 (within 0.20 cm3 of each
       other). 23.30 is also within 0.20 of 23.20 but the tightest pair is
       23.15 and 23.20. Accept 23.15, 23.20, and 23.30 as concordant if
       within 0.20 cm3.

   (ii) Mean = (23.20 + 23.15) / 2 = 23.175 ≈ 23.18 cm3
       (If using three: (23.20 + 23.15 + 23.30)/3 = 23.22 cm3 — either
        approach acceptable depending on concordance criteria.)

(d) Moles NaOH = 0.100 x 0.0250 = 0.00250 mol
    Mole ratio H2SO4:NaOH = 1:2
    Moles H2SO4 = 0.00250 / 2 = 0.00125 mol
    Volume H2SO4 = 23.18 cm3 = 0.02318 dm3
    Concentration = 0.00125 / 0.02318 = 0.0539 mol/dm3

(e) Any two of:
    - Parallax error: read burette at eye level, bottom of meniscus.
    - Not rinsing burette with acid: rinse with the solution it will contain.
    - Adding titrant too fast near endpoint: add dropwise near the colour
      change.
    - Indicator contamination: repeat without indicator.
    - Air bubbles in burette: ensure no air bubbles before starting.

QUESTION 2: QUALITATIVE ANALYSIS (MMO + ACE) — 10 marks
================================================================================

You are provided with two solid samples, P and Q. Carry out the following
tests and record your observations and inferences.

SOLID P:
(a) (i) Carry out a flame test on solid P.
     Observation: Yellow flame. [1]
     Inference: Sodium ion (Na+) present. [1]

(a)(ii) Add dilute nitric acid to solid P. Test any gas evolved.
     Observation: Effervescence; gas turns limewater milky. [1]
     Inference: Carbonate ion (CO3 2-) present. [1]

(a)(iii) Identity of P: Sodium carbonate (Na2CO3). [1]

SOLID Q:
(b)(i) Add dilute nitric acid to solid Q, then add silver nitrate solution.
     Observation: White precipitate. [1]
     Inference: Chloride ion (Cl-) present. [1]

(b)(ii) Add aqueous sodium hydroxide to a solution of Q, then add excess.
     Observation: Light blue precipitate; insoluble in excess NaOH. [1]
     Inference: Copper(II) ion (Cu2+) present. [1]

(b)(iii) Identity of Q: Copper(II) chloride (CuCl2). [1]

(c) Explain why dilute nitric acid is used to acidify the solution before
    adding silver nitrate in test (b)(i). [1]

ANSWER (c): Hydrochloric acid must not be used because it contains chloride
ions which would give a false positive white precipitate with silver nitrate.
Nitric acid does not interfere because all nitrates are soluble.

QUESTION 3: PLANNING (RATE OF REACTION) — 6 marks
================================================================================

Plan an experiment to investigate how temperature affects the rate of
reaction between sodium thiosulfate and hydrochloric acid.

Na2S2O3 + 2HCl -> 2NaCl + S + H2O + SO2

The reaction produces a yellow precipitate of sulfur, which makes the
solution cloudy.

(a) State the independent variable, dependent variable, and two controlled
    variables. [2]

(b) Describe the procedure. [3]

(c) State one safety precaution. [1]

--- ANSWERS ---

(a) IV: Temperature of the reaction mixture (e.g. 20, 30, 40, 50, 60C).
    DV: Time taken for a cross beneath the flask to disappear (s).
    CVs: Volume of Na2S2O3 solution, volume of HCl, concentration of both
    solutions, same flask, same cross drawn on paper.

(b) Procedure:
    1. Measure 50 cm3 of sodium thiosulfate solution using a measuring
       cylinder. Pour into a conical flask placed over a cross drawn on
       paper.
    2. Heat the sodium thiosulfate to the desired temperature using a water
       bath. Use a thermometer to check.
    3. Measure 10 cm3 of dilute HCl using a measuring cylinder.
    4. Add the HCl to the flask and immediately start the stopwatch.
    5. Look down through the solution at the cross. Stop the timer when the
       cross is no longer visible.
    6. Record the time taken.
    7. Repeat at different temperatures (30, 40, 50, 60C), keeping all other
       variables constant.
    8. Repeat each temperature and calculate the mean time.

(c) SO2 gas is produced — it is irritating to the respiratory system. Work
    in a well-ventilated room or fume cupboard. Wear goggles. Do not inhale
    the gas.

QUESTION 4: ENERGY CHANGES / CALORIMETRY (MMO + ACE) — 7 marks
================================================================================

A student investigates the neutralisation reaction between hydrochloric acid
and sodium hydroxide.

(a) The student mixes 50 cm3 of 1.0 mol/dm3 HCl with 50 cm3 of 1.0 mol/dm3
    NaOH in a polystyrene cup. The temperature changes from 22.0C to 28.6C.
    Calculate the energy released.
    (c = 4.2 J/g/C; assume 1 cm3 = 1 g) [3]

(b) Calculate the enthalpy change per mole of water formed. [2]

(c) Suggest two ways to improve the accuracy of this experiment. [2]

--- ANSWERS ---

(a) Total mass = 50 + 50 = 100 g
    Delta T = 28.6 - 22.0 = 6.6C
    q = m x c x Delta T = 100 x 4.2 x 6.6 = 2772 J = 2.772 kJ

(b) Moles of HCl = 1.0 x 0.050 = 0.050 mol
    Moles of NaOH = 1.0 x 0.050 = 0.050 mol
    Moles of H2O formed = 0.050 mol (1:1 ratio)
    Delta H = -2.772 / 0.050 = -55.4 kJ/mol
    (Negative = exothermic)

(c) Any two of:
    - Use a lid on the polystyrene cup to reduce heat loss to surroundings.
    - Use a digital thermometer for more precise temperature readings.
    - Plot a cooling curve and extrapolate back to the time of mixing.
    - Insulate the cup further (e.g. wrap in cotton wool).
    - Stir continuously for even heat distribution.

QUESTION 5: WATER OF CRYSTALLISATION (MMO + ACE) — 5 marks
================================================================================

A sample of hydrated magnesium sulfate, MgSO4.xH2O, has a mass of 5.50 g.
After heating to constant mass, the remaining anhydrous magnesium sulfate
has a mass of 2.70 g.
(Mr: Mg = 24, S = 32, O = 16, H = 1)

(a) Calculate the mass of water lost. [1]

(b) Calculate the moles of anhydrous MgSO4 and the moles of water. [2]

(c) Determine the value of x and write the formula of the hydrated salt. [2]

--- ANSWERS ---

(a) Mass of water = 5.50 - 2.70 = 2.80 g

(b) Mr(MgSO4) = 24 + 32 + (4 x 16) = 120
    Moles of MgSO4 = 2.70 / 120 = 0.0225 mol
    Mr(H2O) = 18
    Moles of H2O = 2.80 / 18 = 0.1556 mol

(c) x = moles H2O / moles MgSO4 = 0.1556 / 0.0225 = 6.92 ≈ 7
    Formula: MgSO4.7H2O
